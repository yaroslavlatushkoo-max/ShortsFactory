# Integration limitations

This document separates what is really implemented from what is only planned or
explicitly unsupported. The integration is a working vertical slice, not a
general-purpose animation compiler.

## Text understanding

- The text parser is **rule-based**, not an LLM. It supports the demo vocabulary:
  Milo/Nina, the glowing orb, and the verbs notice/look, approach, take, hand
  over, surprise, thanks, leave.
- It does not understand arbitrary prose, new character names, new props,
  negation, complex temporal clauses, multiple scenes, or free-form dialogue
  attribution beyond the first quoted line.
- Unsupported input fails with `IntegrationError`; it does not invent data.
- Story Studio's own `StoryPlanner` is not used for arbitrary text: its models
  and validation are reused, but its built-in story is the fixed crystal/Leo/
  Mira demo and would not preserve the user's characters.

## Story Studio coverage

- The generated Project has one scene and one static two-shot camera plan. Scene
  transitions, multiple camera shots, audio events and music from
  `global_audio` are not produced.
- Story Studio validation runs and its errors are fatal. Warnings are preserved
  in the combined report.
- `continuity_requirements` are minimal; the generated project does not attempt
  full continuity analysis beyond Story Studio's built-in checks.

## Animation Lab coverage

- Lab owns kinematic planning and validation, but the Factory storyboard only
  receives **linear x movement**, holds, timings and interaction events.
- Lab `bezier3`, `arc` and multi-waypoint trajectories are not forwarded to
  Factory. The adapter uses `linear` / `hold` trajectories. This is the reason
  the QC check `curved_trajectories` is `NOT_CHECKED`.
- Lab tracks a prop at the **owner's position**, not at a hand point. Factory
  re-attaches the object to the rig's `hand_r` and verifies the actual hand
  distance in rendered frames. The two representations are not identical.
- The Lab validator's DOUBLE_OWNERSHIP check only catches two different owners
  at the exact same timestamp; it does not catch a repeated pickup later without
  a release. The generated timeline has a single pickup and one transfer.
- Collision checking is primitive circle/proximity logic, not full silhouette or
  mesh collision.
- Lab output contains no skeletal joint or finger controls. Factory controls
  poses and a small set of rig points; no finger-level animation is claimed.

## Factory renderer coverage

- The renderer supports the artistic `milo` and `nina` looks and the `orb`
  object. Other characters/props are rejected by the adapter rather than
  rendered as mannequins.
- `compile_storyboard` does not copy a `point` field from a generic `reach`
  action into action parameters; only take/give/drop events receive a point.
  The integration therefore uses `hand_r` for the receiver. A `hand_l` receive
  is not honoured by the current compiler.
- The `exit` action is clamped to x=0.16 (Milo) / x=0.84 (Nina) so the
  characters stay inside the framing check. They walk to the entrance edge but
  do not fully disappear off-screen. True off-frame exit is not implemented.
- The final voice is Piper TTS generated by the existing `build_voice_track`.
  Lip-sync is the existing envelope-based Factory check; it is not phoneme-level
  viseme animation.
- Audio music/ambience from Story Studio is not passed to the renderer. The
  Factory procedural audio and voice track are used.
- The existing pose-quality and framing checks are kept at their original
  thresholds. They are not weakened to make this pilot pass.

## QC coverage

- `PASS`, `WARNING`, `FAIL` and `NOT_CHECKED` are distinct. `NOT_CHECKED` makes
  the overall status `WARNING`, never `PASS`.
- Silhouette intersection is not reliably computable from the current Factory
  API; it is explicitly `NOT_CHECKED`.
- Curved trajectory forwarding is not implemented; it is explicitly
  `NOT_CHECKED`.
- Speed/acceleration thresholds belong to the Lab. The Factory report adds
  decode, determinism, motion, attachment, framing, pose and lip-sync checks.
- The integration report does not claim that the Lab simulation itself is a
  finished cartoon. The MP4 is produced by the Factory renderer and is the
  evidence for the visual result.

## Environment and compatibility

- The three projects are not merged. Story Studio is executed by a separate
  Pydantic-capable Python; the Lab is executed by its own venv. On another
  machine, those paths or environment variables must be supplied.
- No dependencies were installed into another project's environment. The
  Factory venv still has no `pydantic`, and the Lab venv still has no
  `shortsfactory` or Piper.
- The new CLI command leaves all previous commands unchanged. Existing pilots
  and user files are not overwritten; `--overwrite` is required for a non-empty
  integration output directory.
- There is no git history in any of the three projects, so no commit was made.
  A SHA-256 baseline manifest was written before changes.
- Network access and paid APIs are not required.

## Planned, not implemented

- general natural-language story understanding;
- multi-scene stories and transitions;
- curved/waypoint motion forwarded into the Factory renderer;
- full rigid-body or articulated physics;
- reliable silhouette intersection QC;
- full off-screen exits;
- left-hand receive and per-finger controls;
- Story Studio music/ambience integration.
